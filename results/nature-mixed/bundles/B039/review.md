## Final Meta-review

The paper proposes IterSurvey, a framework for automatic literature survey generation that replaces the traditional one-shot paradigm with a recurrent outline generation approach. The system iteratively retrieves papers, generates structured 'paper cards' that distill each paper into contributions/methods/findings, and progressively refines the survey outline. A global review-and-refine loop enhances textual coherence and integrates multimodal elements like figures and tables. The paper also introduces Survey-Arena, a pairwise evaluation benchmark that ranks machine-generated surveys against human-written exemplars. Experiments on established and emerging topics show improvements over baselines in content coverage, structural coherence, and citation quality, with the system approaching human-level quality in 60% of test topics.

### Strengths

- The iterative/recurrent outline generation paradigm is a novel and well-motivated departure from existing one-shot approaches, inspired by human research workflows.
- Paper cards provide a clean mechanism for evidence grounding that demonstrably improves citation recall without sacrificing precision.
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation including multiple baselines, human evaluation, ablation studies, and a novel pairwise benchmark (Survey-Arena).
- The Survey-Arena benchmark is a useful contribution that addresses known limitations of absolute scoring in LLM evaluation.
- Clear writing and good organization with helpful visualizations.
- The generalization test on survey-lacking topics provides evidence of robustness for emerging research areas.
- Ablation study clearly demonstrates the individual contribution of each proposed component.

### Weaknesses

- Quantitative gains over strong baselines are modest (e.g., 4.75 vs 4.66 average content score), raising questions about practical significance.
- Human evaluation uses only 7 experts and compares against just two baselines (AutoSurvey and SurveyForge), limiting statistical power.
- The meta-evaluation of Survey-Arena shows only moderate correlation with citation counts (Spearman's ρ = 0.410), suggesting the benchmark's reliability is not fully established.
- Generation uses only GPT-4o-mini; results may not generalize across different model capabilities.
- Some implementation details are underspecified (e.g., exact similarity threshold τ, stopping criteria parameters, keyword expansion mechanism).
- The computational cost of consulting 1000-1200 papers with iterative retrieval is not discussed, which could be a practical limitation.
- The ablation study uses only 5 topics, which may be insufficient for robust statistical conclusions.
- The figure/table integration component is described but lacks thorough quantitative evaluation.

### Questions

- How was the similarity threshold τ for outline stability determined, and how sensitive are results to this hyperparameter?
- What is the computational cost and time required for generating a single survey compared to baselines?
- How were the 7 human experts selected, and what was their domain expertise relative to the evaluated topics?
- Could you provide more detail on the keyword expansion mechanism - how does the model decide which new directions to explore?
- How does the system handle contradictory information across different papers in the paper cards?
- What is the variance in performance across the 20 topics? Are there topic types where IterSurvey underperforms?
- How do the three judge models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Haiku, GLM-4.5V) agree with each other in the automatic evaluation?
- In the Survey-Arena meta-evaluation, the Spearman correlation of 0.410 with citation counts seems moderate—how does this compare to the correlation of human judgments with citation counts?
- For the survey-lacking topics, IterSurvey shows lower coverage than SurveyForge (4.42 vs 4.50). Why does the iterative approach underperform on coverage in this setting?
- The paper cards are generated by the same model that writes the survey. How do you control for potential bias or hallucination introduced in the card generation step?
- Could you provide more detail on the paper-section relinking mechanism? How is the association determined and what is its accuracy?
- In Survey-Arena, why were LLM judges used instead of human judges? Have you validated the LLM judge agreement with human pairwise preferences on a small subset?
- Can you provide more details on how the figure/table quality is assessed? What percentage of generated visualizations pass the automatic checks, and what are common failure modes?
- Does the performance improvement hold when using a different backbone model (e.g., Claude or Llama) instead of GPT-4o-mini?
- In the ablation study on 5 topics, what is the variance across topics? Are there topics where a component hurts performance?

### Limitations

- The generated surveys may contain citation errors or factual inaccuracies inherent to LLM generation, which the authors acknowledge but do not fully mitigate.
- The evaluation is limited to computer science topics from arXiv; generalization to other domains is untested.
- The Survey-Arena benchmark's moderate correlation with citation counts suggests it may not fully capture survey quality.
- Potential over-reliance on LLM judges for evaluation, despite efforts to use multiple models.
- The system's iterative retrieval process could potentially introduce bias toward papers that are easier to retrieve rather than most relevant.
- Potential negative societal impact: automated survey generation could be misused to create superficially plausible but inaccurate summaries, potentially misleading readers; the paper does not deeply discuss mitigation strategies.
- The computational cost of the iterative process (consulting 1000-1200 papers) may be prohibitive for some users.
- The paper does not deeply analyze failure cases or error propagation risks in the iterative process.
