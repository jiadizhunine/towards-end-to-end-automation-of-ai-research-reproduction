## Final Meta-review

The paper proposes Vision-EKIPL, a reinforcement learning framework for enhancing visual reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The core idea is to incorporate high-quality actions generated by external auxiliary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro) into the GRPO training process alongside actions sampled from the policy model itself. After reward-based ranking, the top-G actions are selected to guide policy optimization. Experiments on three visual reasoning tasks (visual counting, structure perception, spatial transformation) using Qwen2-VL 2B/7B models show improvements over standard RL methods like Reason-RFT, with up to 5% gains on out-of-distribution benchmarks. The paper also reports faster training convergence and provides analysis (Pass@K, action source dynamics) suggesting the method expands the reasoning boundary compared to standard RL fine-tuning.

### Strengths

- Novel and well-motivated idea: addressing the known limitation of standard RL fine-tuning (reasoning boundary constriction) by injecting external expert knowledge into the training process.
- Comprehensive experimental setup: three diverse visual reasoning tasks, multiple model sizes (2B/7B), and in-domain/out-of-domain evaluations.
- Useful analyses: training efficiency curves, action source dynamics (Figure 4), and Pass@K comparisons (Figure 5) provide insight into the method's behavior and claimed benefits.
- The paper acknowledges API costs and practical limitations, showing awareness of real-world constraints.
- Clear motivation and generally well-structured presentation with helpful figures.

### Weaknesses

- Major theoretical inconsistency: The appendix introduces importance sampling (using ratio π_new/q) to justify off-policy samples, but the actual implementation (Eq. 3) uses the standard GRPO ratio π_new/π_old without any importance weights. This disconnect between theory and practice is not addressed and undermines the soundness of the method.
- The top-G filtering introduces selection bias that is not accounted for in the theoretical analysis, further weakening the theoretical foundation.
- Performance gains are modest and inconsistent: improvements over baselines are often only 1-3%, with the 'up to 5%' claim being selective rather than consistent across benchmarks.
- Missing key ablations: no variation of the number of external models (M), no analysis of group size (G) sensitivity, no comparison with simpler distillation approaches (e.g., SFT on external model outputs), and no isolation of the effect of sample diversity vs. external knowledge quality.
- Reliance on expensive proprietary APIs (GPT-4o, Gemini) limits practical applicability, reproducibility, and scalability; no detailed cost-benefit analysis is provided.
- No statistical significance testing is reported despite claims based on 3-run averages.
- Limited comparison with other external-knowledge-infusion methods in RL training; the relationship to distillation is not clearly delineated.

### Questions

- In practice, do you apply the importance sampling correction (ratio π_new/q instead of π_new/π_old) when computing the policy gradient? If not, what is the empirical impact of ignoring this correction?
- How is the KL divergence penalty computed when using external samples? Is it computed relative to the reference policy for all samples, or only for policy-model samples?
- The top-G filtering introduces selection bias. Can you provide a rigorous analysis of how this bias affects the gradient estimates? The appendix discusses IS but doesn't address the selection step.
- Have you compared with a simpler baseline that just samples more actions from the policy model (e.g., N=16 with only policy samples)? This would isolate whether the improvement comes from external knowledge or just having more candidate actions.
- How sensitive are the results to the choice of external models? Would open-source models of similar capability provide comparable gains?
- What is the computational overhead of sampling from external models during training? How does this factor into the overall training efficiency comparison?
- For the action source analysis (Fig 4), how do you determine which actions are selected for the update group? Is it purely based on reward ranking?
- Could you provide error bars or statistical significance tests for the main results, given that you only report averages over 3 runs?
- Why not simply use the external models' outputs as supervised fine-tuning data (distillation) instead of the more complex RL formulation? What specific advantages does the RL approach offer over this simpler alternative?
- What happens if the external auxiliary models are weaker than the policy model? Does the method still provide benefits, or could it potentially degrade performance?

### Limitations

- The method depends on access to high-quality external models, which may not be available in all settings, and the reliance on proprietary APIs (GPT-4o, Gemini) raises reproducibility concerns.
- The theoretical foundation for using external samples with top-k filtering in GRPO is not rigorous; the importance sampling framework is discussed but not actually applied in the implementation.
- API costs for external models can be substantial for large-scale training, and the paper does not provide a detailed cost-benefit analysis or explore cheaper alternatives.
- The evaluation is limited to three visual reasoning tasks; generalization to other multimodal domains is not demonstrated.
- No analysis of failure cases or scenarios where the method might underperform compared to standard RL approaches.
- Potential negative societal impacts, such as the environmental cost of training with multiple large models or the risk of propagating biases from proprietary models, are not thoroughly discussed.
