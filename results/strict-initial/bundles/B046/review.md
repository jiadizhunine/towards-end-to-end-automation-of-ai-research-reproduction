## Final Meta-review

The paper introduces InSTA, a fully automated pipeline for generating web-navigation training data at internet scale without human annotations. It consists of an LLM task proposer that annotates ~150k safe websites with agentic tasks, LLM agents that execute these tasks in a shared virtual browser, and an LLM judge that filters successful trajectories. The authors train Qwen3-1.7B agents on the resulting data and report a 56.9% success rate on a held-out InSTA test set, outperforming the 235B data-collection policy and approaching Gemini 2.5 Flash, with zero-shot transfer to WebVoyager. Code, models, and data are released.

### Strengths

- Scales web-agent training data generation to 150k live websites, far exceeding prior human-annotated datasets and demonstrating a practical path toward internet-scale data.
- A small 1.7B model trained on automatically generated data outperforms much larger models on the authors' test set and transfers zero-shot to WebVoyager, highlighting the data's value for distillation.
- The pipeline is fully automated and includes safety filtering, task proposal, trajectory collection, and judge-based filtering, with component-wise evaluations on safety, judge accuracy, and reliability.
- The paper provides a thoughtful cost analysis and releases code, models, and the dataset, supporting reproducibility and further community research.
- The finding that LLM judge confidence correlates with accuracy is practically useful for scalable selective evaluation.

### Weaknesses

- The primary evaluation relies on an LLM judge that is the same model family used for data generation and filtering, and it is validated on only 100 human-annotated trajectories (82.6% accuracy). No human evaluation of the final trained agent is reported, so absolute success rates remain uncertain.
- The held-out InSTA test set is generated by the same task proposer and judged by the same LLM family used to filter training data, creating a distribution shift that favors models trained on InSTA; transfer to WebVoyager mitigates but does not eliminate this concern.
- There are inconsistencies in the data collection description: Section 5.2 says a fine-tuned Qwen3-1.7B collected 150k trajectories, while Section 6.1 uses 20k trajectories from a Qwen3-235B policy; the relationship between these datasets and which drives the reported gains is unclear.
- Safety and reliability evaluations are based on small hand-picked samples (100 sites for safety, ~300 tasks for reliability), giving wide error bars; the false-negative rate on the full 1M-site crawl is not characterized.
- No controlled comparison is made against other automatic data-generation pipelines (e.g., PAE, NNetNav) or against agents trained on similarly scaled human or synthetic data, making it difficult to isolate the contribution of specific design choices.
- Several implementation details are redacted (exact prompts, hyperparameters, unsafe-site lists), limiting reproducibility from the submission itself.

### Questions

- What is the human-verified success rate of the final Qwen3-1.7B agent on a random sample of held-out tasks, rather than judge-assigned scores?
- How do the 150k trajectories collected with the fine-tuned Qwen3-1.7B in Section 5.2 relate to the 20k Qwen3-235B trajectories in Section 6.1, and which dataset produces the best final agent?
- How was the 100-site safety test constructed, and what are confidence intervals for the reported accuracy, precision, and recall?
- Can the authors provide overlap statistics showing that the training sites do not overlap with WebVoyager/Mind2Web/WebLINX test websites, considering potential content overlap beyond exact URL matches?
- Would the main conclusions change if the held-out test set were evaluated by a judge that was not involved in data filtering or by human annotators on a larger sample?
- What are the exact WebVoyager success rates with standard deviations for all compared models, and were the differences statistically significant?

### Limitations

- The evaluation is dominated by LLM judges with only small-scale human validation, and judge errors (up to 17.4%) propagate into both training-data filtering and final performance estimates.
- The safety filter is tested on only 100 curated websites, so its precision/recall on the full internet-scale distribution is unknown; harmful or illegal tasks may slip through.
- The pipeline excludes account-required, social media, transactional, and state-modifying tasks, limiting the diversity of real-world web interactions.
- Automated interaction with 150k live websites raises legal and ethical concerns regarding robots.txt compliance, website terms of service, and potential disturbance to live services.
- The dataset may still contain PII or copyrighted content despite scrubbing, and no residual PII analysis is provided.
- Despite collecting 2.2M screenshots, the trained model is text-only, so the multimodal value of the dataset is not demonstrated.
