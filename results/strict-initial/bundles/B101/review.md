## Final Meta-review

The paper presents StyleAR, a method for style-aligned text-to-image generation using multimodal autoregressive (AR) models. The key contribution is a data curation strategy that converts the problem from requiring text-image-to-image triplet data to binary text-image training: stylized images are generated by a diffusion model (InstantStyle), but the reference style image is discarded, leaving only prompt-stylized-image pairs. The model uses a frozen CLIP image encoder with a perceiver resampler to produce style tokens, injects Gaussian noise to mitigate content leakage, and at inference applies SAM-based feature subtraction (style-enhanced tokens) to further reduce leakage. Training mixes raw and stylized images in a 1:3 ratio, and DPO post-training is used to improve prompt adherence. Experiments compare against diffusion-based baselines on CLIP-T, CLIP-I, DINO, and a user study, showing competitive performance. Ablations validate the data ratio, style-enhanced tokens, and DPO.

### Strengths

- Addresses an underexplored problem: enabling autoregressive models for style-aligned generation, which is typically diffusion-based.
- The binary-data training strategy avoids the need for costly triplet data, and the mixing of raw and stylized images is empirically shown to improve style consistency.
- Style-enhanced tokens using Gaussian noise injection and SAM-based feature subtraction provide a novel and reasonable mechanism to reduce content leakage.
- Includes extensive comparisons with several diffusion baselines, with both quantitative metrics and a user study, and shows competitive performance.
- The framework is flexible, allowing additional structural conditions (e.g., depth maps) and DPO post-training.

### Weaknesses

- Evaluation is very limited: only 10 style images, 20 prompts, and 800 generated images, with no standard benchmarks or statistical significance tests.
- No direct comparison with existing autoregressive style/personalization methods, weakening the novelty claim of being the first AR approach.
- The training data is still generated by a diffusion model (InstantStyle), so the style diversity and quality are inherently bounded by that generator; the claim of avoiding the diffusion upper bound is overstated.
- There is a fundamental train/inference mismatch: during training, style tokens are extracted from the same image the model is trained to predict, while at inference they come from a different reference style image; this gap is not analyzed.
- Key implementation details are missing: exact training loss, token sequence construction, perceiver resampler training, LoRA target modules, DPO VLM and hyperparameters, and values of alpha and gamma.
- CLIP-I and DINO metrics can be inflated by content leakage; the authors argue this for IP-Adapter but do not provide leakage-adjusted metrics for StyleAR.
- The paper has numerous writing and formatting issues, including incomplete references, duplicated text, and unclear descriptions, harming reproducibility.

### Questions

- How does the model generalize to unseen styles not present in the 80-style training set? Is there any few-shot adaptation capability?
- What is the exact training objective and sequence format? Please specify the loss, the token order, and how the perceiver resampler is trained jointly with the AR model.
- Why not train with the SAM-based style-enhanced tokens directly instead of only using them at inference? What is the impact of Gaussian noise strength gamma and residual ratio alpha, and were they ablated?
- Can you provide quantitative comparisons with existing autoregressive style-transfer or personalization methods (e.g., references [31], [44])?
- How is 'content leakage' quantified beyond qualitative inspection and indirect CLIP-I/DINO changes?
- What VLM was used for DPO preference labeling, and how were chosen/rejected pairs constructed to capture style consistency rather than only semantic alignment?

### Limitations

- Small-scale evaluation on a limited set of styles and prompts; no standard style-transfer benchmark is used.
- Dependence on a diffusion-based generator for stylized training data constrains style diversity and may inherit biases.
- The train/inference mismatch may cause the model to rely on content features from the reference image, leading to content leakage.
- No code, data, or trained models are released, making the work difficult to reproduce.
- Potential negative societal impacts, such as unauthorized imitation of copyrighted artistic styles, are not discussed.
