## Final Meta-review

The paper empirically investigates how large language models (LLMs) update knowledge when encountering non-contradictory (novel) versus contradictory (counterfactual) facts. Using COUNTERFACT facts and GPT-2 small/XL/GPT-J, it reports that non-dissonant updates preserve unrelated knowledge relatively well, while dissonant updates cause catastrophic forgetting of unrelated facts, even with only 10-100 facts. It introduces a selective plasticity scheme that freezes or updates specific neurons based on historical gradients, which helps retention for non-dissonant updates (98% vs 93%) but fails for dissonant updates across all strategies. Finally, it trains simple classifiers on internal activations/gradients or output probabilities to distinguish known, novel, and contradictory facts, achieving >95% accuracy, and proposes this as a protective detection mechanism. The paper argues for append-only, context-preserving updates inspired by human cognition.

### Strengths

- The paper identifies a clearly formulated and important research question: how LLMs handle contradictory vs. non-contradictory updates, which is largely ignored in existing continual learning and model editing literature.
- The experiments are systematic, using 5-fold cross-validation, multiple model scales (GPT-2 small, GPT-2 XL, GPT-J-6B), and multiple update strategies (full finetuning, LoRA, and selective neuron targeting).
- The finding that contradictory updates catastrophically interfere with unrelated knowledge, while non-contradictory updates do not, is a striking and well-demonstrated empirical result within the tested settings.
- The selective-plasticity study is thoughtfully designed, evaluating distinct neuron-selection strategies (plastic, stubborn, candidate, specific, random) and showing a clear asymmetry between non-dissonant and dissonant updates.
- The dissonance detection experiments provide a practical and promising direction: using simple internal features to flag contradictory facts before they cause damage, achieving high accuracy.
- The paper frames its findings in a biological/cognitive context (cognitive dissonance, selective plasticity), which is both interesting and motivating for future research.

### Weaknesses

- The central claim that dissonance specifically causes catastrophic corruption is confounded: dissonant updates required more epochs to converge, so additional training steps (not contradiction per se) could explain the larger forgetting; no equal-compute control is reported.
- The paper relies on the assumption that COUNTERFACT facts are known/unknown without per-sample verification; some 'non-dissonant' facts may be known and some 'dissonant' ones may not be strongly encoded, undermining the clean dichotomy.
- Selective plasticity benefit for non-dissonant updates is largely matched by random neuron selection, which suggests the benefit could be due to parameter sparsity rather than the proposed stubborn/plastic distinction.
- Dissonance detection results may reflect dataset artifacts: novel facts generated by GPT-4 use invented names/relations, making them easy to distinguish from real factual statements; no evidence on realistic, long-form contradictions.
- No comparison with standard continual-learning mitigations (replay, EWC, regularization) for the dissonant scenario, so the claimed impossibility of protection is not strongly established.
- Retention is measured only on a small tracked set of 2,000 facts; for larger models this tracked set is a tiny fraction of model knowledge, so the true extent of interference with broader pretrained knowledge is not quantified.
- No mechanistic or theoretical explanation is provided for why contradictory updates cause catastrophic interference, limiting the generality and actionability of the findings.

### Questions

- Did you run an equal-compute control where non-dissonant and dissonant updates use the same number of epochs/steps? If not, can you attribute the additional forgetting to contradiction rather than longer training?
- How did you verify that each counterfact was actually known by the model before the update, and that each 'new' fact was unknown? Does the conclusion change if you filter by pre-update accuracy?
- Why did random neuron selection perform almost as well as plastic-neuron selection for non-dissonant updates? Would a random-sparsity baseline with the same number of updated weights reproduce the 98% retention?
- Could the high detection accuracy be explained by surface-level distinctions between GPT-4-generated novel facts and real COUNTERFACT facts? Have you controlled for token frequency, name entropies, or factual plausibility?
- How do established continual learning methods (e.g., EWC, replay, GEM) fare on the dissonant update task? Would they also fail catastrophically?
- Are the selective-plasticity experiments performed on GPT-J-6B, or only on GPT-2 models? If not, does the scale-invariance claim for the failure of selective plasticity rest only on GPT-2 models?

### Limitations

- Experiments cover only simple factual statements in English from a single dataset (COUNTERFACT); more complex knowledge types, procedural knowledge, or long-context contradictions may behave differently.
- The evaluation measures accuracy on a small tracked set (2,000 facts), which may not reflect total model knowledge; interference on untracked knowledge is unmeasured, and the tracked set may not be representative.
- Dissonance detection was tested on balanced artificial triples, not on realistic naturally occurring contradictions, which are often noisy, implicit, and context-dependent.
- The paper does not propose or evaluate a concrete mechanism (e.g., append-only memory) to avoid contradictions; the proposed protective mechanism is only a detection classifier.
- The findings could be dual-use as a way to craft misinformation attacks that degrade LLM knowledge, a risk not fully addressed.
- Results are limited to models up to 6B parameters; today's larger LLMs may behave differently.
